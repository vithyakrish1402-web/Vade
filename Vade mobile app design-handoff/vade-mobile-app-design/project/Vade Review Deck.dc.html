Design review · August 2026

Vade

A private messenger where nothing readable sits on your screen until you ask for it.

Mobile · Desktop · Design system · Handoff

The product was signalling security everywhere, so it signalled nothing.

Colour did no work

Emerald sat on buttons, badges, avatars, icons and rules alike. Verification looked like everything else.

Chrome over content

Cards inside cards inside tabs. On a 390px screen the message was the smallest thing on it.

States were missing

No empty, offline, queued or failed states. Key changed looked like a badge, not a warning.

The one idea

Monochrome everything, so one green can mean something.

Green appears in exactly four places: verified, revealed, unread, and enrollment complete. Nowhere else. When you see it, the app is telling you something about privacy.

Reading is a deliberate act

Per message, never per conversation. Opening a thread reveals nothing.

Your gesture, set up on this device. A mismatch says only that it did not match.

It protects itself

No animation on the way out. Instant, so no motion extends exposure.

Plaintext exists only inside that window — not in previews, notifications, clipboard, tooltips or hidden markup.

Trust has four states, and each has a word

Verified Safety numbers compared. Quiet by design — the normal case should not announce itself.
Unverified Encrypted, not yet confirmed. Stated once, never nagged.
Key changed Ochre banner under the header with one action, plus a flag on the list row. Persists until resolved.
Offline Compose freely; messages queue and say so. Nothing is silently lost.

Setup asks for one thing

A shape you can repeat. Everything else happens without the user.

Step 1
Explain

Why a gesture and not a passphrase, in one sentence.

Step 2
Draw

One continuous stroke on a nine-dot grid. Short flicks rejected.

Step 3
Confirm

Repeat it once. Three misses offers a simpler shape instead of a lockout.

Step 4
Choose the look

Pick a protection style with live previews, then start messaging.

Identical rules

Same reveal window, same protection styles, same tokens. Nothing forks.

What we are asking for

01 Tokens and primitives Two palettes, Figtree, pill controls. Replace slate and emerald at the source.
02 Chat surface Bubbles, three protection styles, reveal outline, countdown, receipts.
03 Reveal path Gesture overlay, time-boxed window, automatic re-protect, action sheet.
04 Enrollment and shell Welcome, four-step gesture setup, bottom navigation, list states.
05 Security and desktop Contact security, devices, key-changed handling, then the three-pane web layout.

Four decisions we need

Is the reveal window fixed at six seconds, or user-configurable? The prototype exposes three to fifteen.
Should Illusion decoys come from a fixed local pool, or a per-conversation seed? The second is more convincing and leaks a little structure.
Archive and mute are drawn on the swipe row but have no backend. Cut them from v1, or build them?
Group conversations are out of scope in these designs. Confirm that holds for the first release.
